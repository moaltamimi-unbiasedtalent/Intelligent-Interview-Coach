## 1. Current architecture assessment
Reused as is: the Opportunity domain (`opportunities`, owner-scoped, optional JD document, company fields, `OpportunityRepository.get`), candidate evidence (`document_claims` with review state, `candidate_stories`), the Company Intelligence service (deterministic claims with `FACT`, `REVIEW`, `MODEL_INFERENCE` kinds and `SourceRef` provenance), the existing structured-output builder (`build_structured_response_format`, `parse_structured_output`), the output guard (`security.inspect_output`), injection screening (`security.detect_injection`, `wrap_untrusted`), governed model routing (`workload_model`, `governed_slug`), the OpenRouter client (which meters itself into the P2-A scope), `ai_operation` admission, the operator pause (`career_ai`), and `EntitlementService` via `plan_state`. No second AI system, provider integration, entitlement authority or interview data path was added. Not wired in P5-A: governed knowledge retrieval (extension point only).

## 2. Product definition
AI-assisted interview preparation for one opportunity. It is not a recruitment service, a hiring decision, a guarantee, a verified employability assessment, a salary service or human coaching, and the Pack, the UI and the Markdown download say so.

## 3. Candidate inputs
The job description linked to the opportunity (owner-scoped extraction), the opportunity fields (role, employer, location, country, notes), the candidate's accepted or edited document claims (rejected ones are never used), Story Bank entries, and the existing Company Intelligence report when the opportunity names a company. Every input may be missing; each missing input is stated in the Pack. Interviews, feedback, readiness reports and analytics are deliberately absent.

## 4. Pack structure (nine sections)
Opportunity overview, company context, role requirements and competencies, candidate evidence alignment, preparation gaps, interview question preparation, relevant achievement examples, prioritized preparation roadmap, sources, assumptions and limitations. Each section carries a status (available, partial, insufficient evidence, not applicable, failed) and bounded note codes.

## 5. Evidence classification and provenance
Every item carries a category: candidate-provided, opportunity-provided, verified-external, knowledge-base, ai-guidance or unavailable. Only a company FACT whose source id resolves to a retained `SourceRef` is placed in the verified-external category, and that category means "source-linked excerpt", not "independently verified" (see section 29); unsourced or unresolvable "facts" are dropped and counted (`unsourced_claims_dropped`). Sources keep title, URL, provider, retrieval and effective dates and the self-reported flag. AI guidance (including deterministic rule-based hints, marked `method`) never carries a citation and is always labelled as not a verified fact. Company inference stays inference.

## 6. Company intelligence integration
The existing service is reused through a caller-owned runner; a failure degrades to "research not run". Reviews are not integrated (as in W5) and never appear. **Dependency:** `eval_company_intelligence` fails on main and still fails. Root cause identified: its `endpoint_gated` check searches `src/api/routes/company.py` for the literal `require_capability(Capability.CURRENT_MARKET_RESEARCH)`, while the route enforces the same capability through `require_entitlement(Capability.CURRENT_MARKET_RESEARCH)` (plus the pause and cost dependencies it also checks). The behaviour is present; the evaluator string is stale. The evaluator was NOT changed and the company section is NOT described as fully qualified until that gate is resolved by a separate, reviewed change.

## 7. Competency alignment
Requirements are extracted deterministically from the job description (bullets or requirement cue words, at most 20, nothing added). Each is matched to the candidate's own evidence by shared significant words and competency tags detected from wording. Three honest states only: supported by your evidence, possible match (verify), no evidence yet. No numeric score, probability or suitability statement exists; alignment is explicitly not an employer assessment.

## 8. Candidate evidence and examples
Examples are the candidate's own verbatim words with their ids; nothing is rewritten into a new achievement and nothing (employer, title, date, outcome, revenue, qualification) is invented. Where evidence is missing the Pack says so.

## 9. Interview preparation and roadmap
One bounded model call produces role-specific questions (with evidence to prepare and follow-ups) and an ordered roadmap, constrained to the given requirement and evidence ids. It does not predict what an employer will ask and assumes no interview date. Roadmap actions come from a fixed set (review requirements, prepare example, practise questions, study company, rehearse role, review feedback, revise example). The plan is a stored artifact: using it needs no further generation.

## 10. Preparation gaps
Gap types are evidence gap, information gap and preparation gap. A "skill gap" is never asserted: absence of uploaded evidence is not proof of a missing skill (stated in the Pack).

## 11. Compensation boundary (P10C)
No salary, range, median, percentile, currency conversion, tax or geographic adjustment is generated or stored. A figure in the job description is not copied into the Pack (tested), and model guidance containing a money pattern or a compensation word is rejected as `unsupported_compensation_claim`. The Pack states that compensation is not included. P10C (Global Career Intelligence and Compensation V2) is a dependency and an optional future integration boundary.

## 12. Global knowledge boundary (P10D)
No claim of country, occupation or language coverage is made (`geographic_and_multilingual_knowledge_coverage_not_assumed_p10d`); geography is never inferred from the interface language. The knowledge-base evidence category and a provider extension point (`PackInputs.kb`) exist and are tested with a synthetic provider, but no production retrieval is connected (`knowledge_base_evidence_not_connected`). P10D is a dependency.

## 13. Source precedence
The candidate-supplied job description is treated as input that describes the opportunity; it does not verify claims about the employer (an explicit assumption). Verified external facts come only from the existing research engine with retained sources. Conflicting information is not merged into one claim: sources stay separate and AI guidance stays in its own category.

## 14. Prompt-injection defences
The job description, opportunity notes, claims and stories are screened with the existing detector before anything else; a BLOCK ends the generation as `input_blocked` before any model call. Untrusted text reaches the model only inside a labelled data-only block with a fixed system rule; the model has no tools and cannot change the schema (strict JSON schema plus Pydantic validation). Company website text never enters the prompt. Authorization, ownership and the provider call decision are enforced in code before the model stage, not by the prompt. Model output is guarded with the existing output inspector. Tests cover injected job descriptions and evidence, ungrounded ids, salary and citation attempts, malformed output and cross-candidate access.

## 15. Structured output and validation
Pydantic models with strict schema; the single model response is validated for schema and grounding (requirement and evidence ids must exist, fixed enumerations, no duplicate questions, no money, no URLs, non-empty). Failure categories are bounded codes (`schema_invalid`, `ungrounded_output`, `unsupported_compensation_claim`, `unsupported_citation`, `empty_output`, `output_blocked`, `provider_error`, `input_blocked`, `interrupted`, `internal_error`, `generator_unavailable`). There is no repair loop, no repair by invention, and no raw output is logged or stored.

## 16. Orchestration and generation lifecycle
Entitlement and opportunity ownership, then admission, then one durable row, then input gathering, screening, deterministic assembly, one model call, validation, persistence. States: generating, completed, failed, insufficient evidence (no job description or no detectable requirements: no provider call). The unique `op_key` makes a replay a no-op; a partial unique index allows one `generating` row per opportunity, so concurrent requests produce one generation (tested with threads). An interrupted `generating` row is marked failed after 15 minutes and cannot block a new generation. A failed regeneration is a new failed revision and never alters a completed one.

## 17. Artifact persistence
Table `preparation_packs`: owner, optional opportunity (SET NULL, so a Pack survives deleting its opportunity; the application also unlinks explicitly because SQLite does not enforce the FK), revision, status, schema version, structured content, bounded generation metadata, failure category. No interview content, transcript, feedback, report, analytics snapshot, prompt or raw model output. Included in the candidate export; deleted with the account and by the candidate's own delete.

## 18. Entitlement, downgrade and re-upgrade
`PREPARATION_PACK_GENERATION_ENABLED = False`. While off, generation is refused before the AI admission dependency (no provider call, no rate-limit slot, no quota, no ownership probing) with a fixed safe message. With the gate on, generation needs an ACTIVE Premium subscription via `plan_state`; Basic, `premium_preview`, forged headers or parameters, localStorage, expired, unknown and unreadable state are `not_entitled`. Reading, listing, downloading and deleting stored Packs is never gated by plan or by the constant. Premium to Basic: saved Packs stay readable and downloadable; new generation and regeneration are denied. Basic to Premium: generation resumes (when enabled) as a new revision and nothing is duplicated or deleted.

## 19. History non-bypass
A Pack reads no interview, feedback, report or analytics data (static test on imports and names, and a test that an interview with a secret marker never reaches the model context or the stored content). It is therefore an independent artifact and needs no history gate. If interview-derived inputs are ever added they need explicit lineage and authorization; that is not done here.

## 20. AI cost accounting and operational protection
Generation runs under the existing `ai_operation("career", "pack_generate")`: pause capability `career_ai`, the `cost_career_user` request bucket (observe mode), and the P2-A ledger (one logical operation, provider attempts, known tokens and cost, unknown never zero; verified end to end with a mocked transport). No new workload, pause capability or migration of the ledger was needed. `pack_generate` maps to no product quota, so no Pack allowance is invented and `USAGE_QUOTAS_ENFORCED` stays False; the quota architecture can take a Pack feature later. **Not covered by usage controls:** company research performed during generation uses the existing research engine (its `cost_research_user` bucket exists but is unapplied, as before P5-A), and Pack generation has no product quota until the owner approves one.

## 29. Final qualification findings and corrections (post-implementation review)
**Company-source grounding.** A retained source id that resolves is not proof that the source supports the statement. Investigation of the research engine (`_ingest`): each `FACT` is built from the cited evidence's own retrieved snippet (`ev.snippet`, at most 600 characters), so the statement is source-linked by construction; engine-level `summary_facts` carry no source id and the Pack drops them. The Pack does not, and cannot, check that the excerpt is TRUE (a self-reported company statement is still the company's claim). Corrections: every such item now carries `support: source_excerpt` and `self_reported`, the Pack adds the standing limitation `company_statements_are_source_excerpts_not_independently_verified`, and the candidate-facing label of the category now reads "From a retained external source (an excerpt, not independently verified)" in all eight locales. The category identifier `verified_external` is kept for stored-artifact compatibility; it must not be read as semantic verification. A test pins that a resolving id with unrelated text is labelled only as an excerpt, that unsourced statements never appear, and that nothing but the source-linked company path can create a verified-external item.

**Unsupported external factual claims.** Paths that can put an external statement in a Pack: (1) source-linked company excerpts (above); (2) deterministic role-relevance hints, labelled AI guidance with no source; (3) model guidance. For (3) three controls were added or confirmed: the model is no longer given the employer name at all, so it cannot be prompted to elaborate company facts; guidance containing a year or an external figure (headcount, size, percentage, customers, locations) is rejected as `unsupported_external_claim`; guidance with a URL or a money pattern was already rejected. Guidance remains labelled "AI-assisted, not a verified fact" and never carries a source. Residual limitation: a model could still state a qualitative falsehood about an employer without a figure; that cannot be detected deterministically, which is why it is labelled and carries no citation. Tests pin each control.

**Generation recovery (15 minutes).** The earlier rule reaped any `generating` row older than 15 minutes from creation, so a legitimately slow generation could in principle have been marked failed and replaced by a concurrent one. Correction: the row now carries a heartbeat (in the bounded `generation` JSON) renewed at every stage boundary (before and after input gathering including company research, before and after the single model call); the recovery rule is "no sign of life for 15 minutes". The longest silent stages are bounded by existing timeouts (model call: 10 second connect and 60 second read per attempt with a few transient retries; web fetches 8 seconds each); the longest silent company-research stage was not measured here. `finish` is single-use, so a late result can never overwrite a recovered row. Tests: lease renewal keeps a three-hour-old row alive and a concurrent request gets `in_progress`; 26 minutes of silence is recovered; the late finish is refused.

**Idempotency, revision uniqueness, owner isolation.** The unique `op_key` is queried together with the owner, so another candidate's identical client key neither collides nor reads (tested). Revision numbers were computed as max plus one: a rare interleaving (a competing generation committing and finishing between the read and the insert) could have produced a duplicate revision, because the one-generating index only arbitrates live rows. Correction: a unique constraint on user, opportunity and revision, plus a bounded recompute-and-retry in `start`. Tested with eight concurrent workers (all revisions unique and contiguous) and a direct duplicate (rejected).

**Migration 0034 database semantics.** The migration was rendered offline for the PostgreSQL dialect (no connection, protected stores untouched): `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_pack_one_generating ON preparation_packs (user_id, opportunity_id) WHERE status = 'generating'`, `CONSTRAINT uq_pack_revision UNIQUE (user_id, opportunity_id, revision)`, `ON DELETE CASCADE` for the user and `ON DELETE SET NULL` for the opportunity. The model renders the same partial index; a test pins both. PostgreSQL treats NULL as distinct in unique indexes, so the two constraints do not bind a Pack whose opportunity was deleted (`opportunity_id` NULL); generation always requires a live opportunity, so this does not affect generation. Because 0034 was unmerged, the revision constraint was added to 0034 itself rather than a new revision. The SQLite behaviour was exercised for real; the PostgreSQL behaviour was verified as DDL only, not executed against a PostgreSQL server.
