# 06G sample-size and power guidance (generated)

Generated by `python scripts/language_human_eval.py guidance --write`; do not edit by hand (a test fails if it drifts). Offline, deterministic, no model call.

This guidance is PER LANGUAGE: each language is analysed on its own and no pooled claim across languages is made (LG-13). The numbers are engineering guidance for the owner, not thresholds (OD-GATE-THRESHOLDS) and not a qualification.

Unit of analysis. The unit is the ITEM (a model output), rated by several raters; an item's rating is the mean of its raters. Ratings of the same item by different raters are not independent, so only the number of ITEMS counts for the intervals below. Rater effects (one rater harsher than another) are not modelled; with three raters the interval is somewhat too narrow, which is why agreement is reported next to it. The spread `sd` of an item's rating across items is unknown until a pilot; 1.0 on the 1 to 5 scale is a typical assumption, 0.8 and 1.2 bracket it. Re-estimate it from the first language rated before sizing the rest.

The 06C sample (30 outputs per language) therefore pins a mean only to about +-0.4 at sd 1.0; the 06G extended sample (60 outputs per language) to about +-0.3. Neither can support a precise per-language claim.

## Items with ZERO events needed to show a rate is below a bound (95 percent, rule of three)

| bound on the rate | items |
|---|---|
| 10% | 30 |
| 5% | 60 |
| 2% | 150 |
| 1% | 300 |

So 60 clean items show only that an unsafe or wrong-language rate is probably below 5 percent; they cannot show it is below 1 percent. One observed event in 60 items already gives a 95 percent Wilson upper bound of roughly 9 percent.

## Agreement sample

Krippendorff's alpha and kappa need variation in the ratings and at least two raters on the same items; at least three raters (the 06G minimum) keep alpha defined when a rater is excluded. The bootstrap interval for alpha resamples items and is wide at 60 items; report it, do not hide it.

## Decision for the owner (OD-BUDGET, OD-GATE-THRESHOLDS)

1. Choose the interval width you need per language (column h) and the spread you assume (row sd); read the items needed. 2. Price that many generated outputs with the 05C governor before approving the budget. 3. Decide the thresholds; the report compares to PROPOSALS only until you do.
